# ECC Copyright and Contributions (Draft)

**Status: internal planning document. Not a copyright assignment or contributor agreement.**

## 1. Rightsholder verification

Before publishing a formal copyright notice, record:

- Full legal name of each rightsholder.
- Which files or contributions each rightsholder owns.
- Evidence of original authorship and creation dates.
- Any employment, contractor, school, client or other agreement that may affect ownership.
- Any assignments or permissions already granted.

Do not treat the repository owner, Git author field or GitHub username as conclusive proof of legal ownership.

## 2. Proposed notice format

Use the following only after the rightsholder and year range are verified:

Copyright (c) [YEAR OR YEAR RANGE] [VERIFIED RIGHTSHOLDER LEGAL NAME]. All rights reserved, except as expressly licensed.

Do not publish the placeholder as though it were a completed notice.

## 3. Third-party materials

Maintain THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES with, for each dependency or bundled asset:

- Component and version.
- Source and copyright attribution.
- Licence identifier and licence text or official reference.
- Required notices, attribution and source-distribution obligations.
- Modifications made, if any.

Audit both backend and frontend dependencies, including transitive dependencies and bundled assets. The direct dependency manifests alone are not a complete inventory.

## 4. External contributions

Before accepting external pull requests:

- Publish contribution rules.
- Require contributors to confirm that they have the right to submit the contribution.
- Select and legally review a CLA or DCO process.
- Make the permission scope explicit, including whether the project may distribute the contribution under both Community and commercial terms.
- Record the contributor's acceptance.
- Review third-party code, generated code and copied snippets for provenance and licence compatibility.

A CLA should not be described as transferring ownership unless it actually does so. A broad licence grant may be sufficient, but the exact wording needs legal review.

## 5. Proposed contribution policy

Until the contribution process is approved, maintainers may ask contributors to open an issue and discuss proposals, but should not merge external code contributions. Do not imply that submitting a pull request automatically grants the project all rights needed for commercial relicensing.

## 6. Records

Keep an auditable record of:

- Original project creation and development history.
- Commit history and authorship.
- External contributions and permissions.
- Dependency and asset inventories.
- Published licence versions and effective dates.
- Commercial agreements and licence grants, stored securely outside the public repository.
